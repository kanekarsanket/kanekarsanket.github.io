Where the numbers come from

Everything on this site traces back to an official statistical release. The charts reach public services that allow direct browser requests and need no account or key.

Live sources
  • Federal Reserve H.6, Money Stock Measures — M1, M2, currency in circulation and their components, monthly since 1959.
  • Federal Reserve H.15, Selected Interest Rates — the federal funds rate, Treasury constant-maturity yields and the bank prime rate.
  • Bureau of Labor Statistics, Consumer Price Index — CPI for all urban consumers, used for inflation and for converting nominal rates to real ones.
  • Bureau of Economic Analysis, National Income and Product Accounts — nominal and real GDP.
  • U.S. Treasury, Fiscal Data — federal debt held by the public, daily since 1997.
A note on FRED

FRED is the natural place to get all of these, and every chart here links to its FRED page so you can download the series in one click. FRED's programming interface cannot be called from inside a web page, though: it requires an account key and its servers do not permit cross-site browser requests. The charts therefore fetch the same official series through DBnomics, a free public mirror of the Fed, BLS and BEA releases, and through the BLS public API. The FRED links are for you; the mirror is for the page.

About

This is an independent teaching resource. The explanations, charts and exercises are original and written for this site. Chapter and section headings follow the standard organisation of an undergraduate money-and-banking course so the material can be read alongside whichever textbook a course uses; no textbook text is reproduced here.
